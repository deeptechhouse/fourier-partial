fourier_partial · user manual

Taking a sound apart

How to use the app and the command-line tool: analysing audio, listening to the pieces, digging under what you can hear, and getting files back out.

Contents

    What this program does

    It takes a piece of audio and splits it into separate tracks, each one a single pitch you can hear on its own. Play them all together and you get your file back, exactly.

    A recorded sound is a mixture. A cymbal is not one thing; it is dozens of tones ringing at once. This program finds those tones, follows each one as it moves and fades, and gives each its own track — so you can mute the ones you do not want, solo the one you do, and export whichever you keep.

    The one promise worth understanding

    Whatever the analysis finds, one track is always called Residual. It holds everything the tones did not account for: breath, hiss, the sharp edge of an attack. It is not a leftovers bin that happens to be roughly right — it is computed as a subtraction: 

    residual = your audio − (all the tones added together)

    Which means the parts cannot fail to add back up. Unmute everything and you are listening to your original file, sample for sample. That stays true no matter how good or bad the analysis was, and it stays true however many passes you run.

    Why some things cannot be separated

    A sound that is sharp in time cannot be sharp in pitch. That is not a limitation of this program but of physics — the same uncertainty principle that governs particles. A click has no pitch to find, so it lands in the residual, and no setting will change that. The residual is where the physics goes, not where the failures go.

    Getting started

    Under a minute either way.

    In the app

    1. Double-click FourierPartial.app. A browser tab opens by itself.
    2. Drag an audio file onto the page, or click to browse.
    3. Wait. A progress bar tells you which stage it is on.
    4. Tracks appear, one row each, with a waveform and a name like G3 or A4 + 3 harmonics.
    5. Press Play. Use M and S on any row to mute or solo it.

    Accepts WAV, FLAC, AIFF, MP3 and more, mono or stereo, up to ten minutes. Nothing is uploaded anywhere — the program runs on your machine and the "server" is your own computer talking to itself.

    On the command line

    Put it on your PATH once:

    $ ln -s "$(pwd)/fourier-partial" /usr/local/bin/fourier-partial

    Then, from anywhere:

    $ fourier-partial one take.wav
# writes take_wav.zip next to take.wav

    Or a whole folder at once:

    $ fourier-partial many ./recordings -o ./out

    Without the symlink, run it by path — /path/to/fourier-partial one take.wav — or as python3 -m backend.cli from the project folder.

    How long it takes

    Roughly real time on a modern machine: a one-minute file takes a few seconds, a ten-minute file around a minute and needs a couple of gigabytes of memory while it works. Raising Passes multiplies that — see Looking more than once.

    Listening to the pieces

    The point of the track list is to answer a question by ear: what is this sound actually made of?

    Reading a row

    What you seeWhat it means
    G3One tone, near the note G3.
    A4 −12cTwelve cents flat of A4. A cent is a hundredth of a semitone.
    C5 + 3 harmonicsA fundamental with three of its harmonics, bundled as one voice.
    ResidualEverything the tones did not explain. Always present.
    · L2Found on the second pass. Only appears if you used more than one.

    Rows are grouped by pitch, so a long recording reads as "G4, seven times" rather than seven near-identical rows. Click a group to open it.

    Mute and solo

    • Mute silences that track. Everything else keeps playing.
    • Solo silences everything else. Solo several and you hear only those.
    • A track that is both muted and soloed is silent — mute wins, because it is the more specific instruction.
    • Clear solos in the transport puts everything back.
    Try this first

    Solo the residual on its own. It is the fastest way to hear what the analysis could not explain, and it tells you immediately whether your settings are finding the material you care about or leaving it behind.

    The two views

    Each row draws either the amplitude (how loud, over time) or the frequency trajectory (how the pitch moved). The trajectory view is the one that shows you vibrato, glides and tape wobble as shapes. Toggle it in the transport bar.

    Changing how it listens

    Fourteen settings, grouped by the stage they affect. Nothing here is a quality dial — most of them are choosing a side of a trade.

    In the app

    The Settings panel sits above the drop zone. Move anything and the Re-analyse button lights up; the file stays loaded, so you can compare settings without re-importing. Show advanced reveals the rest, and Reset to defaults puts everything back.

    Only settings you actually changed are sent. That matters more than it sounds: a ten-minute file gets cheaper defaults chosen after its length is known, and sending every value would override them and make the file unanalysable.

    On the command line

    $ fourier-partial settings              # every setting, with ranges
$ fourier-partial one take.wav -s window_size=8192 -s peak_threshold_db=-75

    -s repeats. Values outside a setting's range are clamped to the nearest legal one and a warning is printed, rather than failing — so a script sweeping a range does not die at step twelve.

    Presets

    Six ship with the program. They set only a few values each; everything else stays default.

    PresetForSets
    Sharp attacksPlucks, drums — where the hit matters more than the pitchwindow 1024, shortest tone 20 ms
    Sustained and tonalDrones, bowed strings, held voicewindow 8192, shortest 150 ms, drift 40c
    Bells and struck metalInharmonic material, where each partial is interesting alonewindow 8192, harmonic tol. 10c
    Voice and glidesSinging, portamento, tape wobbledrift 200c, gaps 5
    Dig deepEverything findable, including the junkthreshold −85 dB, no cull, 300 tracks
    Just the notesA handful of usable stems, not a surveythreshold −40 dB, cull 3%

    Save your own at the top of the Settings panel. Changing any slider clears the preset name — it stops claiming to be a preset the moment the values stop matching. 

    $ fourier-partial presets
$ fourier-partial one bell.wav --preset "Bells and struck metal"

    The four settings that matter most

    Window size — the one true trade-off. Bigger separates pitches that sit close together and smears attacks; smaller sharpens attacks and blurs pitch. You cannot have both. Start here.

    Quiet-tone threshold — the most direct "more tracks / fewer tracks" control. Lower finds quiet detail and more junk.

    Shortest tone kept — your main defence against hiss arriving as notes. Raise it when the track list fills with nonsense.

    Harmonic tolerance — how far from an exact multiple a tone can sit and still join the same note. Piano and bells are genuinely stretched, so being strict loses real harmonics.

    Full reference

    Looking more than once

    One pass does not find everything, and not because the analyser is weak. A tone only counts if it is loud enough relative to the loudest tone in the same moment — so anything hiding under something louder is never picked up at all.

    Take the loud thing away and the quiet one becomes the loudest thing left. Analyse again and it is found. That is all these two settings do.

    SettingFlagWhat it does
    Passes--depth N Analyse N times, each pass working on what the last could not explain. You keep every pass's tracks.
    Layers to strip first--strip N Run N passes and throw their tracks away, keeping only what they could not explain. Then start. The "what is underneath?" control.

    Try it

    tracks you keep thrown away not yet explained
    The one catch, and it is important

    Passes keeps the promise: every pass's tracks plus the final residual still add back to your original file, exactly. Stripping cannot — you threw those layers away, so what is left sums to the stripped signal instead. That is the point of the setting, not a bug, and the program never pretends otherwise: the manifest inside a stripped export says THESE FILES DO NOT SUM BACK TO THE ORIGINAL RECORDING and explains why.

    What to expect

    On a test signal — one loud tone with two much quieter ones underneath — a single pass finds one track. Four passes find thirteen, including both hidden tones, and the reconstruction is still exact.

    Passes stop early on their own if a pass finds nothing: the residual comes back unchanged, so every further pass would repeat it. You are never charged for depth that had nothing to do. 

    $ fourier-partial one cymbal.wav --depth 3
$ fourier-partial one cymbal.wav --strip 1 --depth 2   # skip the obvious layer

    In the app both live in the How many times it looks group. "Layers to strip first" is behind Show advanced.

    Getting files out

    Six ways, which sound like more than they are. Pick by what you want to do next.

    Every track as audio One WAV per track, zipped. Adds back to your original. Export all → WAV
    -f wav
    Every track, no residual The tones only. Will not reconstruct — the noise is missing. Export all → WAV, tones only
    -f wav-tones
    Just what I am hearing, as one file A bounce of the current mix with mutes and solos applied. Export all → Record out, one WAV
    -f wav-mix
    Just what I am hearing, separately Only the audible tracks, one file each. Export all → Record out, separate WAVs
    Notes for a synth One MIDI file per track, with pitch bend so glides survive. Export all → MIDI
    -f mid
    One MIDI file for everything Every track on its own channel in a single file. Export all → MIDI, single file
    -f mid-single

    Record out

    The What you're hearing group in the Export all menu. Its labels update as you mute and solo, so it always states what it would capture — "3 of 4 tracks". Mute everything and it disables rather than writing silence.

    The command line has no mutes to read, so use --tones-only to drop the residual, or -f wav-mix for a single summed file.

    Things worth knowing

    • Bulk WAV exports are 24-bit. The reconstruction is exact in 32-bit float; 24-bit rounds each sample by an inaudible amount. Export a track on its own for bit-identical float.
    • The residual has no pitch, so it is never exported as MIDI. Nothing is wrong if you get 39 MIDI files where there were 40 WAVs.
    • Every archive contains a MANIFEST.txt saying what is inside, what was left out and why, and whether the parts add back up.
    • MIDI pitch bend is set to ±48 semitones. If your instrument ignores that, wide glides play flattened.
    • A ten-minute analysis is around 3.2 GB of WAV. The app tells you the size on the button before you commit.

    When something looks wrong
    
      
    Most of these are the program behaving correctly in a way that is easy to misread.
    

      
    
        
    
          
    What you seeWhat is happening
    
          
            
    
              Hundreds of meaningless tracks
              Noise is being tracked as tones. Raise Shortest tone kept, or raise Quiet-track cull. The "Just the notes" preset does both.
            
    
            
    
              Only two or three tracks
              The threshold is culling real material. Lower Quiet-tone threshold, or try "Dig deep". If the material is quiet underneath something loud, raise Passes instead — that is a masking problem, not a threshold one.
            
    
            
    
              Almost everything is in the residual
              Usually correct. Percussive and noisy material genuinely has no pitch to find. Try a smaller Window size if the sound is rhythmic.
            
    
            
    
              A vibrato note shattered into fragments
              The pitch moved further between looks than allowed. Raise Allowed pitch drift, or use the "Voice and glides" preset.
            
    
            
    
              A bell became one track, not many
              Its partials were gathered into one "note". Lower Harmonic tolerance, or use "Bells and struck metal".
            
    
            
    
              MIDI has fewer files than WAV
              Correct. The residual has no pitch and is never written as MIDI.
            
    
            
    
              The exported tracks do not add back up
              You used tones only (the residual is missing) or strip (layers were discarded). Both are documented in the archive's MANIFEST.txt.
            
    
            
    
              Analysis is extremely slow
              Check Passes — it multiplies the whole analysis. Then Step between windows, which is the main cost dial.
            
    
            
    
              The app opens an old-looking page
              A stale browser tab. Reload it. If a copy is already running, a second launch may bind a different port.
            
    
            
    
              "Unsupported audio format"
              The file's actual bytes are checked, not its extension. A renamed or truncated file is rejected here.
            
    
              
        
    
      
    

      
    
        A settings recipe that usually works
        
    
          Start with the preset closest to your material. Change one
          setting, re-analyse, and listen to the residual. If what you want is in the
          residual, the analysis is missing it; if what you want is spread over forty
          junk rows, cull harder. Only reach for Passes once you are
          sure the material you want is being masked rather than filtered.
